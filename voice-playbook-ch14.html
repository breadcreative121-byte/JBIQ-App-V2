Purpose & Authority

This chapter closes the playbook. It defines the metrics every previous chapter ladders to, the QA regression suite that gates releases, the change process for the playbook itself, the exception system, and the governance cadence. It is the chapter that turns the rest of the playbook from a document into a living instrument.

It exists to:

  • Map every chapter's acceptance criteria to a single, instrumented metrics dashboard.
  • Make Daily Engaged Conversations (DEC) — the GA north star — the primary success measurement.
  • Codify the regression suite that gates every model release.
  • Define the change process for the playbook itself.
  • Lock the exception criteria — what justifies a documented deviation.
  • Set the governance cadence — per-release, nightly, weekly, quarterly.
  • Make versioning and migration of the playbook predictable.

This chapter is normative. A metric that's not measured cannot be governed; a change that bypasses the process is a structural defect.

01

Scope & Non-Goals

1.1 In scope

  • The DEC north star and the metrics that ladder to it.
  • Response-shape, voice disclosure, language, reliability, trust, repair metrics.
  • The QA regression suite — what runs on every model update.
  • Acceptance per chapter — the cross-chapter dashboard structure.
  • The change process — propose, review, approve, communicate.
  • Exception criteria — what justifies documented deviation.
  • Versioning — minor vs major bumps, migration notes.
  • Governance cadence — per-release, nightly, weekly, quarterly.

1.2 Out of scope

  • Specific dashboard tooling and data pipeline architecture (governed by Data & Analytics).
  • Per-pod OKR setting (governed by Pod Plans).
  • Commercial KPI tracking (governed by Business).
  • Per-language Localisation parity tooling (governed by Localisation).
02

Governance invariants

  • Every chapter's acceptance criteria ladder to instrumented metrics. Metrics that are not instrumented are not governed.
  • Every metric has an owner, a target, a measurement method, and a cadence.
  • DEC (Daily Engaged Conversations) is the primary success metric. Task completion, satisfaction, retention, language parity, and trust metrics all ladder to it.
  • The QA regression suite runs on every model release. Suite failure blocks the release.
  • Section 2 invariants in every chapter are non-negotiable; their violation in production triggers automatic rollback and post-incident review.
  • The playbook is a living document. Changes follow the §13 process. In-place updates within minor versions are permitted; structural changes require version bumps.
  • Exceptions are documented in writing, time-bounded, and reviewed quarterly.
  • Quarterly governance review is held on schedule with all chapter owners + Trust & Safety + Compliance + Legal as standing attendees.
  • Every measurement, every classifier, every regex rule is reviewed quarterly for accuracy and drift.
  • Metric breach > 10% over a 7-day rolling window pages the chapter owner; a Section 2 invariant violation pages the playbook owner + Compliance.

04

The north star — DEC

DAILY ENGAGED CONVERSATIONS

DEC = DAU × Conversations/User/Day. Target at year-1: ≥ 2 sessions/user/day at 25%+ DAU/MAU. Every chapter's metrics ladder to it — engagement is what the playbook is for.

DEC is the GA primary metric. It is computed daily, broken out by domain, by intent shape, by language, by signature experience. The dashboard is live by Beta exit; per Data & Analytics, freshness is 5 minutes.

The QA regression suite

The QA regression suite runs on every model release. Failure on any check blocks the release. New chapters and new rules add to the suite; rules are not retired without a documented exception process (§13).

11.1 What runs on every release

  • Forbidden-phrase regex suite (all of Ch. 13 §3).
  • Forbidden-opener / closer regex (Ch. 3 §6.2, §7.2).
  • AI self-reference classifier (Ch. 1 invariant 2).
  • Secrets regex on logs / memory / ledger / adapter traces (Ch. 8 §7).
  • Prohibited-pattern classifier suite (Ch. 4 §10, Ch. 13 §4–§10).
  • Cross-modal anchor-match audit (Ch. 10 §5).
  • Compliance Ledger write success rate on synthetic commit-class flows (Ch. 8 §9).
  • Cinematic-experience persona consistency on the GA cinematic set (Ch. 5 §12).
  • Self-harm content hard-block test set (Ch. 12 §8.3, Ch. 13).
  • DPDP memory delete propagation synthetic check (Ch. 7 §13).
  • Cross-language tone-classifier delta across production languages (Ch. 6 §4).
  • Voice latency benchmark on a canonical intent set (Ch. 5 §15).
  • Sourced-verdict compliance audit on verdict-rendering surfaces (Ch. 11 + Ch. 13).

11.2 What runs nightly

  • Sampled production replies × tone-classifier × intent-shape stratification.
  • Sampled production voice transcripts × four-beat classifier.
  • Sampled refinement events × chip-vs-question classifier.
  • Sampled commitment-class flows × commitment-state audit.
  • Cross-modal desync audit on sampled voice + UI turns.
  • Sensitive-mode classifier × upsell-regex intersection check.
  • Surveillance-language classifier on sampled day-0-warm-start turns.
  • Convention-regex audit (₹, lakhs, DD/MM, am/pm, km).
  • Per-language match-rate sample × code-switch handling.

11.3 What runs weekly

  • Cross-surface consistency comparison.
  • Cross-language CSAT delta.
  • Guardrail FNR / FPR on labelled test sets.
  • Per-partner SLA health + cold-start budget audit.
  • Helpline directory accuracy.

11.4 What runs quarterly

  • Blind-attribution calibration panel (Ch. 1 §10).
  • Principle-weighted critique calibration panel (Ch. 2 §16).
  • Allowed-emoji vocabulary review (Ch. 1 §6).
  • Bharat tier-2 / tier-3 usability panel (Ch. 6 §13).
  • Helpline directory comprehensive review (Ch. 12 §6.3).
  • Cinematic-experience identity check (Ch. 5).

The change process

The playbook is a living document. Changes follow a defined process so the team can evolve the rules without losing coherence.

13.1 Propose

  • Any team member may propose a change to the playbook.
  • Proposals are written: title, rationale, affected chapters, proposed text or table change, impact assessment on existing chapters and metrics.
  • Worked examples are required — at least one positive and one negative.

13.2 Cross-functional review

Reviewers from Product, Design, Engineering, AI, Content, Localisation, Compliance, Trust & Safety — depending on which chapters the proposal touches. Reviews are async by default; a synchronous review is called when reviewers disagree.

13.3 Impact assessment

  • What chapters are affected?
  • What metrics change?
  • What machine specs (classifiers, regex, prompts) need to be updated?
  • What in-flight features or partners are affected?
  • What is the migration plan for existing flows?

13.4 Approval

Chapter owner + playbook owner sign off. For changes that affect commitment authority, security, or Compliance Ledger contracts, Compliance and Legal must also sign off.

13.5 Communicate

  • Change is published with version bump (§15).
  • All chapter owners and downstream pods are notified.
  • Machine specs are updated in parallel.
  • QA regression suite is updated to detect new rules.
14

Exception criteria

Sometimes a rule doesn't fit. Exceptions are permitted but must be documented and time-bounded. The exception criteria:

  • Regulatory or legal requirement in direct conflict with a rule (e.g., RBI-mandated flow that doesn't fit the response anatomy).
  • Technical constraint making compliance infeasible, with documented mitigation.
  • User research evidence that the rule produces worse outcomes in a specific context.
  • Partner-specific obligation that overrides a default (rare).

All exceptions are time-bounded (default 6 months), documented in writing, revisited quarterly, and either renewed, resolved, or terminated.

15

Versioning

Versioning is semver-style at the playbook level.

  • Major bump (V1 → V2) — structural change. New chapters, new invariants, deprecated rules. Migration notes required. Cross-team announcement.
  • Minor bump (V0.1 → V0.2) — additive change. New sub-sections, new metrics, GA additions. No deprecation of existing rules.
  • Patch bump (V0.2.1) — clarification, typo, calibration update. No rule change.
  • Every chapter carries its own version that may be bumped independently within the playbook major version.
  • Machine specs (classifiers, regex, prompts) are versioned alongside the playbook. Drift between the playbook and the machine specs is a structural defect.
