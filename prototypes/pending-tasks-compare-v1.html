Pending tasks — how should we show 1 vs many?

Four representations of the Home “Return · pending task” state, each shown with one pending task and with seven. Same real tasks in every version (recharge, electricity, gas, LIC, FASTag, JioMart cart, doctor follow-up), same JBIQ design system. Tap chips, expanders, dots — they’re live.

Reading it: the question isn’t just “which looks best with 7” — it’s how gracefully each degrades to 1, since most days a user has 0–2 pending things, not 7.

Control · current

1 · Horizontal carousel

What the prototype ships today: each task is a full card; you swipe / tap dots between them. (Auto-advance turned off here so it’s fair to judge.)

Rare in the wild — closest: Venmo / CRED action carousels.
Trade-off: only 1 of 7 is ever visible; the other 6 are hidden behind a gesture that NBU / T2–T3 users often don’t discover. No sense of “how much is on my plate”.

2 · Most-urgent + “N more”

Lead with the single most urgent task as the full context card; collapse the rest into a “+6 more” row that expands to a compact list, each with one action.

Pattern: Asana “A task is due today” + Greenlight stacked actions. Asana · Greenlight
Trade-off: one clear primary action (voice-first friendly) and a visible count. Slight risk the “more” list is ignored — mitigated by the count being on the button.

Alternative

3 · Count summary

Lead with the number — “7 things need you today” — and a one-line breakdown by kind. Tap to expand the full list. Collapses cleanly to “1 thing needs you”.

Pattern: Apple Reminders count tiles + Monzo “You have 4 notifications”. Reminders · Monzo
Trade-off: best “sense of load” at a glance; but adds a tap before any action, and a bare number can feel like a chore-list rather than help.

4 · Grouped — “up to date, except…”

Reassure first: most categories are handled; only the ones needing action carry a badge. Borrows the “you’re up to date” calm from Airwallex.

Pattern: Airwallex “My tasks” grouped with per-group badge. Airwallex
Trade-off: emotionally calm and scannable; but needs a category model, and with only 1 task the reassurance framing can feel heavier than the task itself.

The research — how other apps show pending tasks

Real screens behind the four options above, grouped by pattern. Two questions drove the scan: how apps surface one thing that needs you, and how they handle many. Each shot links to its Mobbin source.

When there’s ONE

A single dedicated action card

The consistent move: icon → what it is → when it’s due → one primary button, usually dismissable. This is essentially JBIQ’s existing context-line + chips — it holds up well.
