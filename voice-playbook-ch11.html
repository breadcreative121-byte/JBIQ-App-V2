Partner ecosystem & attribution

00

Purpose & Authority

This chapter governs how third-party tools — Swiggy, Apollo, IRCTC, partner skills inside JBIQ — show up inside the JBIQ conversation without breaking the canonical contract. It defines what partners declare, what JBIQ governs, the five resolution strategies for when multiple partners can serve the same intent, the ranking signal hierarchy, the equity rules that keep the ecosystem open, and the transparency surfaces the user always has access to. With GA, it also governs Vertical Lift — the internal agentic build pipeline that takes a validated use case to a live signature experience in 7 days — and the Partner Portal move from advisory to hard-gate.

It exists to:

  • Make partner integration a translation problem, not an integration problem.
  • Lock the canonical contract — partners declare capability, JBIQ governs interaction.
  • Codify the five resolution strategies for multi-partner intents.
  • Prevent silent partner monopolisation through equity rules.
  • Make ranking decisions inspectable, correctable, reversible by the user.
  • Govern the Vertical Lift agentic pipeline (GA addition).
  • Lock the Partner Portal hard-gate at GA — 30-min sandbox-to-skill, 5-min behaviour visibility, 1-min error / violation surfacing.

This chapter is normative. A partner integration that violates the manifest contract, renders custom UI, embeds checkout, writes directly to memory, or hides attribution must be rejected at registry time or revoked at runtime.

02

Ecosystem invariants

  • Every partner tool that participates in JBIQ conversation must register a valid manifest. The manifest is the only contract surface between partner and JBIQ.
  • Partners declare canonical intents. They do not invent new ones, do not introduce new states, do not introduce new output primitives.
  • Partner output is rendered through canonical primitives only. Custom partner UI is prohibited.
  • JBIQ owns commitment authority regardless of which partner fulfils the intent. Partners do not display their own checkout or confirmation surface.
  • Memory writes occur only through JBIQ-mediated paths declared in the manifest. Direct partner writes are prohibited.
  • Partner branding appears only as attribution. It does not appear as chrome, frame, or surface header.
  • Cross-partner data exchange via JBIQ is prohibited unless explicitly user-mediated.
  • OTPs, PINs, full card numbers, Aadhaar, biometric data, CVV values never appear in any manifest field, adapter spec, or partner-bound payload (Ch. 8 §7).
  • Resolution outcomes must be deterministic given the same inputs and signal state.
  • Every partner-fulfilled output renders with partner attribution (per Manifest §4.2). Attribution is non-negotiable regardless of strategy.
  • Default partner selection must be explainable in plain language via the "Why this result?" surface.
  • Partner ranking may not be commercially manipulated without disclosure.
  • A single partner cannot exceed the monopoly cap in a domain without explicit user opt-in.
  • Suppressed partners must be visible to the user on demand.
  • New partners receive a cold-start exposure budget.
  • Demotion signals are time-decayed; partners are not banished forever for a single failure.
03

The Partner Capability Manifest

The manifest is the declarative contract a partner submits. It describes what the partner's tool does, what data it consumes and produces, what risk it carries, what locales it serves. JBIQ does the rest. The Manifest is the only point of negotiation; everything that follows in the system is derived from what is declared there.

05

The adapter DSL

Adapters translate the partner's raw response into a canonical primitive schema. JBIQ defines two adapter types: declarative (recommended) and imperative (fallback).

5.1 Declarative adapters

  • JSONPath expressions for source field selection.
  • A bounded transform vocabulary: format, concat, default, conditional, localise, currency, distance, datetime.
  • Output is always a canonical primitive schema; validated at registry time.
  • No executable code, no embedded scripting, no HTTP calls within the adapter.
  • Stateless — same input, same output, every time.

5.2 Imperative adapters (controlled fallback)

  • Partner registers an adapter_endpoint; JBIQ POSTs the raw partner response and the endpoint returns canonical primitive schema.
  • Used only when the response cannot be expressed declaratively.
  • Carries extended latency budget, mandatory security review, mandatory sandbox period before promotion to verified.

5.3 Worked adapter fragment

DECLARATIVE ADAPTER — SWIGGY.SEARCHRESTAURANTS → DISCOVERYVIEW (CAROUSEL)

items_path: $.restaurants

field_map:

title ← $.name

subtitle ← $.cuisineCsv

anchor.distance ← $.distanceKm

badges ← [$.rating styled as rating, $.etaMinutes styled as eta]

image ← $.bannerUrl

action ← drill targeting tool swiggy.getMenu with restaurantId from $.id

The same shape, with different field paths, expresses apollo.searchDoctors and irctc.searchTrains. The primitive does not change. The system does not grow.

06

Commitment authority

THE RULE

JBIQ owns COMMITMENT_REQUIRED regardless of which partner fulfils the intent. Partners declare commitment_level and risk_tier in the manifest. JBIQ runs the canonical CommitmentSummaryView, brokers UPI / payments through the JBIQ payment broker, captures provenance to the Compliance Ledger, then dispatches the partner execute call with a tokenised payment reference. The partner never sees the UPI PIN.

  • Partner declares commitment_level: none, soft, hard-functional, hard-financial.
  • Partner declares risk_tier: low, medium, high (Ch. 8 §3, §5).
  • Partner may declare auth_floor higher than the tier defaults, never lower.
  • Partner may declare reversibility_window_seconds for soft / Semi-Auto flows.
  • Partner never renders their own checkout. Partner never sees PIN / OTP / full card. Partner never logs payment credentials.
  • Compliance Ledger writes are JBIQ-side and fail-closed — the partner action does not dispatch until the Ledger has written (Ch. 8 §9.3).

The five resolution strategies

When multiple partners can serve the same canonical intent, JBIQ chooses a resolution strategy. Each strategy has a use case, primitive compatibility, and transparency requirement.

StrategyUsePrimitive compatibilityExample
Single-BestLow-stakes, high-frequency. Choice fatigue would hurt UX.Any primitive."Translate 'thank you' to Tamil" → one translation. Attribution + 'Why this?'.
MergedDiscovery and List primitives where breadth is the value.DiscoveryView, ListView, ComparisonView."Restaurants near me" → Swiggy + Zomato + MagicPin intermixed by ranking, per-item badge.
StratifiedWhen merging would lose value (cross-partner pricing for the same item) or partner identity materially shapes the result.DiscoveryView, ComparisonView."iPhone 15 price comparison" → Amazon, Flipkart, Croma each in their own labelled section.
Choice-GatedHigh-stakes actions where partner identity materially affects outcome and the user has known preferences.Any primitive, typically gating a downstream CommitmentSummaryView."Book a cab" → Ola, Uber, BluSmart as a picker; user selects; query dispatched.
Sequential-FallbackExecution-only tools where partner identity does not affect outcome value.StatusView for outcomes; void_render for state mutation."Send an OTP" → try primary SMS gateway → fall back to secondary → surface only success.
08

Ranking signal hierarchy

Ranking signals are applied in fixed order. Higher signals dominate lower ones; lower signals act as tie-breakers within bands of higher-signal equivalence.

#SignalDefinition and decay
1Explicit user preferenceUser-set partner default for an intent class. Never decays unless user changes. Override-only.
2Autonomy-reinforced preferenceSuccessful repeat completions in this intent class with this partner. Decays per Memory Model time-weighting (60-day half-life by default).
3Domain contextActive assistant lens biases candidates within that domain. Session-scoped.
4Locale match scorePartner's declared locale strength for the user's session locale.
5SLA health scoreRecent latency and availability vs declared SLA. Rolling 7-day window.
6Result quality scoreItem count, freshness, ratings density. Per-query.
7Partner equity adjustmentCold-start budget for new partners. Monopoly cap dampening for over-represented partners.
8Disclosed commercial signalLowest weight. Permitted only if disclosed in the user-facing transparency surface. Undisclosed bias is an invariant violation.

Demotion signals are time-decayed. The half-life of demotion is shorter than the half-life of promotion to avoid overcorrection from a single bad day.

09

Partner equity rules

Without equity rules, the autonomy reinforcement loop entrenches incumbents. New partners never receive enough traffic to demonstrate quality. The four equity rules keep the system open.

9.1 Cold-start budget

A newly verified partner receives an exposure budget that artificially boosts their ranking for a bounded number of invocations or time window. The budget is consumed regardless of outcome, but quality outcomes accelerate transition into autonomy-reinforced ranking.

9.2 Monopoly cap

No single partner may exceed a defined fulfilment share for a canonical intent within a session window unless the user has explicitly opted in. The cap preserves discoverability of alternatives even as autonomy reinforces a strong preference.

9.3 Demotion decay

A partner that fails (timeout, error, user correction) is demoted in ranking. Demotion decays over time so a single failure does not banish a partner. Decay is asymmetric — faster than promotion decay — so the system recovers from incidents.

9.4 Suppression visibility

If a partner is filtered out of a candidate set (SLA breach, locale mismatch), the user must be able to see this on demand. Suppression is never permanent without explicit user action.

10

Transparency

LEARNING IS BOUNDED BY TRANSPARENCY

Every learned ranking decision must be inspectable. Every learned preference must be correctable. Every learned default must be reversible. A user who cannot find out why a particular partner was chosen, or who cannot easily change that choice, has lost agency without knowing it.

10.1 Attribution per render

Every partner-fulfilled output renders with partner attribution per Manifest §4.2. Attribution is non-negotiable regardless of strategy. The attribution is the badge, the surface_text ("via Swiggy"), and the partner mark in the card title row.

10.2 "Why this result?"

Every partner-fulfilled output supports a "why this?" query that returns plain-language ranking explanation.

EXAMPLE

"Showing this because you've ordered from Swiggy 12 times in the last month, and they had the fastest delivery estimate for your area."

10.3 Partner Preferences surface

A user-accessible surface listing per-domain partner preferences. Editable. Resettable. Per-domain, never global. Reachable from any partner-fulfilled output via "Why this result?" → "Manage preferences".

10.4 Disclosed commercial influence

Any commercial weighting applied to ranking must be disclosed within the transparency surface. The disclosure must be in plain language, not buried in terms of service. Undisclosed commercial bias is an invariant violation.

10.5 Audit log

Resolution decisions for high-stakes intents are logged in the Compliance Ledger with a reasoning trace: candidate set, applied filters, ranking signals, chosen strategy, fulfilment outcome.

Vertical Lift — the GA-added agentic pipeline

GA ADDITION

Vertical Lift is the internal-build counterpart to the partner manifest. A validated use case becomes a live signature experience in 7 days via agentic build against playbook primitives. Design enters at validation, exits at primitives ship, re-enters at signature-experience review. 2+ net-new signature experiences per month at steady state.

12.1 The 7-day cycle

DayStageOwnerDeliverable
1–2Validated use caseUXR + vertical PMDemand evidence, intent set drafted, success criteria.
3–4Primitives instantiatedDesign (Core Experience + vertical) + AIExisting playbook primitives composed; gaps filled by Design Ops.
5Agent buildVertical Lift agent + AI CoreIntent assembly, eval harness, build scaffolding.
6Eval gateQuality + Localisation + ComplianceQuality bar checked; per-language parity verified; guardrails verified.
7LiveVertical PM + Core ExperienceSignature experience ships; lifecycle home picks it up.

12.2 Primitives the playbook hands to agents

  • Intent set templates per intent shape (Ch. 4 §3).
  • Evaluation harnesses per intent shape and per language.
  • Build scaffolding — response anatomy renderers, refinement chip generators, edge affordance composers.
  • Persona templates for cinematic experiences (Ch. 5 §12).
  • Repair vocabulary per language (Ch. 9 §11).
  • Memory-write declarations and consent-gate templates (Ch. 7).
  • Commitment surface scaffolding (Ch. 8 §4).

12.3 What agents can and cannot do

ActionAgent-permitted?Notes

Introduce a new canonical intent shapeNoCanonical intents are governed by Ch. 4 §3; expansion requires playbook revision.
Introduce a new output primitiveNoCanonical primitives are governed by §4; expansion requires manifest revision.

12.4 Design SLA against the 7-day cycle

  • Design enters at Day 1–2 to confirm the use case maps to existing primitives.
  • Design exits at Day 3–4 once primitives are confirmed and any gaps are filled.
  • Design re-enters at Day 6 to review the agent's render output against the quality bar.
  • Design re-enters at Day 7 for go / no-go signature-experience review.
  • Design does not block the build between Day 4 and Day 6 — the agent runs, the eval gate catches issues, design reviews outputs.
13

Partner Portal — advisory to hard-gate

GA ADDITION

At GA, the Partner Portal moves from advisory to hard-gate governance. 30 minutes from sandbox to first working skill. 80% of governance checks automated. 5-min behaviour visibility. 1-min error / violation surfacing. 9-of-10 partner actions self-serve.

13.1 Portal lifecycle states

Six states (from Partner Capability Manifest §6) — submitted, validating, sandbox, provisional, verified, deprecated, revoked. The Portal surfaces every state to the partner with current SLA, current ranking weight, current violation log, current traffic share.

13.2 Automated governance at GA

  • Manifest schema validation — automated.
  • Adapter spec validation — automated.
  • Locale checks — automated.
  • Compliance gates (data residency, retention, DPDP) — automated where regulation permits, human review where required.
  • Cold-start eligibility check — automated.
  • SLA threshold monitoring — automated; breach surfaces in < 1 minute.
  • Anti-pattern detection — automated regex + classifier suite.

13.3 Manual review at GA

  • Imperative-adapter security review — mandatory before sandbox-to-provisional.
  • Health-domain sensitive-data review — Compliance.
  • Initial trust-tier promotion (sandbox → provisional → verified) — manual at each step.
  • Commercial signal disclosure copy — Legal review.

SEQUENCE

14.5 Vertical Lift cycle — Cricket Match Companion

DAY-BY-DAY

Day 1–2 — Validated use case: second-screen for cricket fans watching JioHotstar. Demand evidence: 5% of JioHotstar Cricket users want a deeper experience.

Day 3–4 — Primitives composed: voice four-beat for live commentary, share-card primitive for moments (wicket, six), friend-group leaderboard surface (new primitive — gap filled by design).

Day 5 — Vertical Lift agent assembles intent set, eval harness, language coverage. Three languages on Day 5 (Hindi, Tamil, English); others queued.

Day 6 — Eval gate: voice commentary latency < 1.5s vs scoreboard; cross-language parity within 10pp; guardrails firing on sensitive moments (player injury, controversial decisions).

Day 7 — Live. Lifecycle home picks it up for users who have engaged with cricket content. WA share-back live for wickets / sixes / match results.
